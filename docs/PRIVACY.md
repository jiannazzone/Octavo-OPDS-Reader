# Octavo Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 5, 2026

Octavo doesn't collect any data. I don't run a server for it, and it has no accounts, ads, analytics or tracking. Nothing you do in Octavo is sent to me.

## On your iPhone

Octavo keeps your books, server addresses, reading places, bookmarks, settings and recent searches on your iPhone. Passwords and sign-ins are kept in the Keychain.

If you back up your iPhone, the backup can include your server addresses, places, bookmarks and settings. Books aren't backed up, and passwords and sign-ins don't move to a new iPhone.

## Sent to your servers

When you add a server, Octavo connects to the address you enter. It sends your sign-in and your searches, and asks for book lists, covers and books. With BookOrbit, it also sends your reading place, your bookmarks and the books you mark finished.

I can't see any of this. Each server's owner decides how it's handled.

A catalog can link to covers or books on other sites. Octavo gets those from there, without your password.

## Other sites

A few links in the app open websites in Safari, such as the source code on GitHub. Those sites have their own privacy policies.

## Apple

If you choose in iOS Settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple may give me crash reports and usage numbers. Apple's privacy policy covers this.

## Deleting your data

- **Sign out** deletes your sign-in from your iPhone. With BookOrbit, it also ends the session on the server.
- **Remove server** deletes that server's sign-in. **Remove books too** leaves nothing of that server on your iPhone.
- **Deleting the app** deletes your books, places and settings. iOS can keep passwords and sign-ins after an app is deleted, so use Remove server first.
- **Anything on a server** is managed on that server. Octavo can't delete it for you.

## Changes

Any changes to this policy will be on this page, and its history is on GitHub.
